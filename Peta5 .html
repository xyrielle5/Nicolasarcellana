LUZON


sisig
Originating from the culinary capital of Pampanga, Sisig is arguably the most famous dish from Luzon. It is traditionally made from boiled, chopped, and grilled parts of a pig's head (like the ears, cheeks, and jowls). The meat is tossed with diced onions, chicken livers, and chili peppers, and usually served piping hot on a sizzling cast-iron plate, often topped with a raw egg and a squeeze of fresh calamansi. It is a beloved pulutan (beer snack) and viand.
Laing
Representing the rich and spicy flavors of the Bicol region, Laing is a creamy, flavorful dish made from dried taro leaves cooked slowly in thick coconut milk (gata). It is heavily spiced with siling labuyo (bird's eye chili) and seasoned with shrimp paste or flaked fish. Simmering the ingredients for over an hour allows the leaves to absorb the rich, savory coconut cream.
Bagnet
From the Ilocos provinces in the north comes Bagnet, the ultimate crispy pork belly. Similar to lechon kawali, the pork belly is boiled with spices until tender, air-dried, and deep-fried in lard until the skin blisters into a perfect crunch while the meat stays juicy. It is traditionally eaten dipped in a mixture of KBL (kamatis, bagoong, at lasona - tomatoes, fermented shrimp paste, and shallots) or plain cane vinegar

VISAYAS


La Paz Batchoy
Hailing from Iloilo, this rich, savory noodle soup features firm egg noodles swimming in a slow-cooked pork broth. It is generously topped with pork bits, liver, crunchy chicharon (pork rinds), fried garlic, and spring onions.
Chicken Inasal -
The Visayan version of barbecue, this Bacolod-style grilled chicken is marinated in a special blend of calamansi, ginger, lemongrass, and annatto (atsuete) oil. This gives the chicken its signature vibrant color, smoky aroma, and juicy texture
Cebu Lechon
Famous nationwide, this whole-roasted pig is known for its incredibly crispy skin and tender, flavorful meat. The pig is traditionally stuffed with a fragrant mixture of lemongrass, star anise, garlic, and scallions, completely eliminating the need for any dipping sauce.

MINDANAO


Pastil
Often described as the ultimate budget-friendly comfort food, this Maguindanaon staple consists of steamed rice topped with shredded, spiced meat (usually chicken or beef). It is neatly wrapped in a banana leaf, which gives the rice a subtle, earthy aroma. It is traditionally eaten on the go and is often paired with a spicy condiment called palapa
Satti
popular breakfast and street food in Zamboanga and the Sulu archipelago, Satti consists of skewered, grilled meats—often chicken or beef—marinated in a blend of rich spices. Unlike typical barbecue, it is served swimming in a thick, fiery-red, slightly sweet, and spicy peanut sauce, and typically paired with puso>
Pyanggang
Hailing from the Tausug people of Sulu, Pyanggang is a distinct and flavorful chicken dish. The chicken is marinated and stewed in a rich paste made of coconut milk, turmeric, ginger, and uniquely—burnt (or toasted) coconut meat, which gives the dish its signature black color and deep, smoky, nutty flavor.
